Overview

As the world becomes increasingly connected, more people are maintaining and forming relationships, pursuing education, and conducting business across international borders. Despite this global connectivity, one challenge persists: coordinating across time zones remains surprisingly difficult.

This case study displays the communication barriers faced by international users, students, remote professionals, and global teams, who rely on everyday messaging platforms to stay connected and organized. While tools like Slack, WhatsApp, Discord, and Teams have transformed how people communicate, they fall short when it comes to scheduling across time zones. Users are left to manually calculate time differences, switch between apps, and hope nothing gets lost in translation — a process that is error-prone and mentally taxing.

Our team set out to understand this pain point at a deeper level by speaking directly with students who navigate these challenges daily. Through user interviews and research, we sought to uncover how timezone friction affects real communication habits and what an ideal solution might look like. The result is a proposed extension or integration for existing communication platforms that surfaces timezone information naturally within the conversation flow, reducing cognitive load and making cross-timezone scheduling feel effortless.

Problem

Staying connected with people across different parts of the world has become increasingly challenging. Everyone manages their own schedules, responsibilities, and timezones, which makes coordinating even a simple conversation more complicated than it should be. As time passes, these small barriers accumulate into missed messages, delayed responses, and difficulty finding mutually convenient moments to talk. Scheduling ahead of time is often the most reliable solution — but that introduces its own questions: Where does the scheduling process begin? When is the right moment to reach out? How do two people decide on a time without creating more back‑and‑forth? This problem highlights the need for a clearer, more intuitive way for users to coordinate availability directly within their existing conversations.

Research

We interviewed three individuals — Marlie (21) from the UK/Netherlands, Eurphie (20) from China, and Takato (20) from Japan — who frequently use communication apps to socialize with friends and family from different time zones. Our interview protocols involved a remote one-hour session and two in-person interviews conducted by Shogo and Sasha.

We asked for their consent and provided context for the goals we were aiming for. We asked questions about their navigation process and obstacles they face. We followed up with questions about specific aspects that caused confusion, asking how it felt, what could be improved upon with the messaging feature, and how it can be improved. We also asked specific questions about certain applications to further understand what works and what does not. The interviews were around an hour each.

Solution

We found that by having the option to add timzones and highlight a user's availablity helps users plan ahead when making plans. This is especially works with the best match system because it suggets the best time immeidately once both users have given their availablity. This reduces the cognitive load of having to calculate time differences and manually coordinate across apps, making scheduling feel more seamless and integrated into the conversation flow.

Discoveries

During this process, we learned many aspects of design and integration that helped use understand why some design are the way they are. One of the biggest takeaways is how much thought and time it takes to intuitively and smoothly communicate information. The interviews have taught me to think in ways I never would have thought and put me in a perspective that I never would see. The more I brainstormed the more ideas and obstacles appeared resulting in forward progressing and regression.

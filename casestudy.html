Scheduling International Communications Made Easy

Back to Portfolio

Overview

Role: UX Researcher & Designer — Team of 4

As the world becomes increasingly connected, more people are maintaining and forming relationships, pursuing education, and conducting business across international borders. Despite this global connectivity, one challenge persists: coordinating across time zones remains surprisingly difficult.

This case study displays the communication barriers faced by international users, students, remote professionals, and global teams, who rely on everyday messaging platforms to stay connected and organized. While tools like Slack, WhatsApp, Discord, and Teams have transformed how people communicate, they fall short when it comes to scheduling across time zones. Users are left to manually calculate time differences, switch between apps, and hope nothing gets lost in translation — a process that is error-prone and mentally taxing. As we conducted our research and user testing over the span of 3 months we sought to understand people's main issues with scheduling across time zones and how we can approach this problem and resolve it.

Our team set out to understand such issues point at a deeper level by speaking directly with students who navigate these challenges daily. Through user interviews and research, we sought to uncover how timezone friction affects real communication habits and what an ideal solution might look like. The result is a proposed extension or integration for existing communication platforms that surfaces timezone information naturally within the conversation flow, reducing cognitive load and making cross-timezone scheduling feel effortless.

Problem

Staying connected with people across different parts of the world has become increasingly challenging. Everyone manages their own schedules, responsibilities, and timezones, which makes coordinating even a simple conversation more complicated than it should be. As time passes, these small barriers accumulate into missed messages, delayed responses, and difficulty finding mutually convenient moments to talk. Scheduling ahead of time is often the most reliable solution — but that introduces its own questions: Where does the scheduling process begin? When is the right moment to reach out? How do two people decide on a time without creating more back‑and‑forth? This problem highlights the need for a clearer, more intuitive way for users to coordinate availability directly within their existing conversations.

Research

We found that people who communciate often with friends or colleagues internationally found it difficult and time consuming to schedule calls and meetings. They described their struggles with weeks of planning and off and on text interaction times. Therefore, we thought to incorporate a time conversion and scheduling system within the Instagram to make it easier for users to coordinate across time zones. By allowing users to share their availability and see overlapping free times, we can reduce the back-and-forth and make scheduling feel more seamless and integrated into the conversation flow. A quote that stood out during our intial interview was "Easier just to have their time next to their username." This helped us with the layout with the time location on the main DM screen and preferance setting. Expanding upon this we also wanted to incorporate a calander view that would allow users to see each other's availability. This created more opportunities for users to find overlapping free times and make schedulting feel more convenient. We thought this would help with easier coordination and hasten the scheduling process.

Solution

During our second user testing found that by having the option to add timzones and highlight a user's availablity helps users schedule ahead when making plans. This is especially works with the best match system because it suggets the best time immeidately once both users have given their availablity. This reduces the cognitive load of having to calculate time differences and manually coordinate across apps, making scheduling feel more seamless. A quote that stood out during our second user testing was "I like the match system because it immediately gives a time suggestion." After creating the calender we thought that by having a system that can suggest the best possibl times for both users to result in immediate planning. This then resulted in the best match system which would suggest the best time immeidately once both users have given their availablity.

Discoveries

During this process, I learned many aspects of design and about user testing. I found difficulties in approaching solutions and executing them effectively and efficiently. One of the biggest takeaways is how much thought and time it takes to intuitively and smoothly communicate information. The amount of revision and interations to get to a final product is much more than I expected. The interviews have taught me to think in ways I never would have thought and put me in a perspective that I never would see. The more I brainstormed the more ideas and obstacles appeared resulting in forward progressing and regression. However, after the entire process I understand why so much planning and testing is needed in order to conduct a strong foundation to use to elavate an idea.
